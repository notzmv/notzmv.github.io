Historical Document
We hold these truths to be self-evident, that all men are created equal.
[wiː hoʊld ðiːz truːðz tuː biː sɛlf ˈɛvɪdənt ðæt ɔːl mɛn ɑːr kriːˈeɪtɪd ˈiːkwəl]

I am writing a new writing system for English using Perso-Arabic script.
[aɪ æm ˈraɪtɪŋ ə njuː ˈraɪtɪŋ ˈsɪstəm fɔːr ˈɪŋɡlɪʃ ˈjuːzɪŋ ˈpɜːrʒoʊ ˈærəbɪk skrɪpt]

Daily Conversation & Plurals
She bought three books, two laptops, and five new pens.
[ʃiː bɔːt θriː bʊks tuː ˈlæptɒps ænd faɪv njuː pɛnz]
شه بات ثرى بوكها، تو لپتاپها، اند فايل نيو پنها.
Nominal plurals systematically take -hā (بوكها, لپتاپها, پنها).
Verbal vs Nominal Contrast
He speaks fast while reading many books.
[hiː spiːks fæst waɪl ˈriːdɪŋ ˈmɛni bʊks]
هى اسپيكس فاست وايل ريدڭ منى بوكها.
Verbal agreement retains Sīn (اسپيكس) while nominal plural uses -hā (بوكها).
Past Tense Morphophonemics

[ʃiː wɔːkt tuː ðə ˈsɪti ænd pleɪd ˈmjuːzɪk fɔːr hɜːr frɛndz]
شه واكت تو ذا ستى اند پلايد ميوزك فور هر فرندها.
Surface past tense -t (واكت) vs -d (پلايد).

2. System Overview & Principles

Aljamiado English is a morphophonemic adaptation of the Perso-Arabic script for the English language, modeled directly after aljamiado/index.org (Aljamiado Português). It prioritizes horizontal rhythm, phonetic precision, and morphological clarity.

5. Morphophonemic Rules

Rule I: Nominal Plural Domain (-hā / ـها)

Plural nouns (-s / -es) attach the graphic logogram -hā (ـها) to eliminate long Sīn tails and distinguish nominal plurals from verbal agreement: books → بوكها, cats → كتها, dogs → داگها.

Rule II: Verbal Agreement & Lexical Sibilants (س / ز)

3rd-person singular present verbs retain explicit phonetic س / ز so they are never confused with plural nouns: he speaks → هى اسپيكس, she runs → شه رنز. Lexical sibilants retain Sīn/Zāy: this → ذس, was → واز.

Rule III: Verbal Past Tense (-d / -t: ـد / ـت)

Past tense -ed uses surface morphophonemic -d (ـد) or -t (ـت): walked [t] → واكت, played [d] → پلايد, wanted [ɪd] → وانتد.

Rule IV: Selective Silent He (ه / ـه)

Long vowels drop silent final e by default (make → ميك, bite → بايت). Silent He (ه / ـه) is reserved for short monosyllabic words (be → به, me → مه, she → شه) or explicit final -es / -as markers.
